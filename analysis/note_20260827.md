### 💡 An unverified channel and an unmeasured lead travel together — ADBE — 08-27

ADBE came up today with `Cached IR URL: None` and a cadence row that named **no advance-PR channel
at all**. That combination is the precondition for the failure mode that cost eight sessions on
TECH: concluding "the PR isn't out" from a channel nobody ever established *exists*. So I checked
whether Adobe issues an advance PR before checking whether one was missing.

It does — on **BusinessWire**, *"Adobe to Announce Q\<N\> FY\<YYYY\> Earnings Results on \<Month D\>"*.
That alone was the fix I went looking for. But measuring the six historical advances to get the
lead turned up something I wasn't looking for: **the stored lead was wrong.**

| Quarter | Advance | Results | Lead |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q3 FY22 | 2022-09-07 | 2022-09-15 | 8d |
| Q4 FY24 | 2024-12-03 | 2024-12-11 | 8d |
| Q2 FY25 | 2025-06-02 | 2025-06-12 | 10d |
| Q3 FY25 | 2025-09-02 | 2025-09-11 | 9d |
| Q1 FY26 | 2026-03-02 | 2026-03-12 | 10d |
| Q2 FY26 | 2026-06-01 | 2026-06-11 | 10d |

The table said **~14d**; it's an **8–10d** band. The old number looks assumed rather than measured
— and left alone it would have sent a session hunting ~4 days early every quarter, finding an
absence that was guaranteed by construction.

**The pattern worth naming: when a cadence row names no channel, the lead in that row is probably
unmeasured too.** Both are symptoms of the same thing — nobody ever watched the symbol through a
full announce→report cycle. They're worth auditing together, because the failure they produce is
identical (a structurally guaranteed absence, misread as a signal) and because the channel check
gives you the observations the lead needs for free.

⚠ The uncomfortable part, for the calibration ledger: **the wrong lead would not have changed
today's outcome.** ADBE gets skipped under 14d and under 8–10d alike. The error was invisible
precisely because it didn't bite — which is how a bad parameter survives indefinitely in a table
that otherwise looks healthy. If gating ever moves into the hook, this is an argument for storing
the *observations* (advance date → release date pairs), not just the derived lead: a lead you can
recompute is a lead you can audit.

### ✅ Yesterday's "CNM cadence break" was my gate, not the company — 08-27

Closing the loop on the 08-26 note. CNM's advance PR landed 08-26 at 16:19 ET and confirmed
**2026-09-09 bmo** — so the lead is **exactly 14 days, five quarters running**. It never broke.

What broke was the gate: the 08-25/08-26 sessions computed the PR's due date as 14 days before
**the DB's then-09-08**, expected it on 08-25, and read one day of absence as a cadence failure —
then reasoned forward from that to cast doubt on the date itself.

**The generalisable bit: gating off an unconfirmed DB date imports that date's error into the
cadence model, and the model then appears to disconfirm the date.** The DB row was off by one and
every downstream inference inherited it. Two cheap defenses, both now in the log: when a lead is
metronomic prefer `PR_due = last_year's_advance + 364d` over `DB_date − lead`, and treat a single
day of absence as noise rather than a break when you're within ±1d of the due date. Either one
would have said "wait a day" instead of "the cadence is broken."
