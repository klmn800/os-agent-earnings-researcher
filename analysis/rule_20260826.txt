- **⚠⚠ `+364d` is only as trustworthy as *that specific quarter's* variance — check the multi-year band for the quarter you're on, not the symbol as a whole. (2026-08-26, CPRT.)** This table carried CPRT as "`+364d` off the 2025-09-04 Q4 release ⇒ 2026-09-03 = DB; **finnhub's 09-09 is a +6d artifact**" — stated with the same confidence used for symbols whose dates really are metronomic. Pulling Copart's full Item 2.02 history showed why that was wrong: its **Q1/Q2/Q3 are tightly anchored, but Q4 ranges Sep 3 → Sep 20** (2025-09-04, 2024-09-04, 2023-09-14, 2022-09-07, 2021-09-08, 2020-09-03, 2019-09-05, 2018-09-19, 2017-09-20). DB's 09-03 is at the **extreme early edge** of that band and 09-09 sits mid-band, so the dissenting date was never an artifact at all — it was an ordinary candidate, and the "artifact" label had quietly converted an unsourced guess into a settled fact. **Practice: before invoking `+364d` (or calling a dissent an artifact), list that quarter's last ~5 actual dates. A tight cluster ⇒ the corroborator is meaningful; a multi-week band ⇒ it is noise and the symbol must wait for a company source.** Fiscal-year-end quarters are the usual offenders — audit/10-K timing moves them in a way Q1–Q3 never move. Companion to the WSM rule above: that one counts observations behind a *lead*, this one counts spread behind a *date*.
- **⚠ An aggregator's "Confirmed" flag is not evidence — and is checkable against structure. (2026-08-26, CPRT.)** investing.com/tipranks both reported Copart Q4 as "Sep 09, **Before Open (Confirmed)**." The date is arguable, but the *timing* is provably false: Copart has furnished Item 2.02 **after the close in 8 of 8 quarters, 16:14–16:27 ET**. A source that is demonstrably wrong on the half you can verify earns no credit on the half you can't. **When a third party volunteers a "confirmed" label, test it against the one field you already have structurally locked (usually bmo/amc from furnish times); if it fails there, discard the whole row.**
